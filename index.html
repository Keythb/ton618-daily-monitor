The trigger was a listing, not a datapoint. China's CXMT closed its Shanghai debut Monday +466% — IPO priced at ¥8.66, closed at ¥49 — for a market capitalisation of ¥3.28tn ($485B), overtaking ICBC as the most valuable mainland-listed company. Turnover exceeded ¥140B, the first mainland stock ever to cross ¥100B in a session.

Now join it to the tape three sessions earlier. On 25–26 July SK hynix signed letters of intent with NVIDIA covering preferential multi-year HBM3E and HBM4 supply, with milestone volume commitments the parties value at up to $500B, plus a 2GW SK Telecom AI cloud built on Vera Rubin. Three sessions later hynix is down 14.7%. Meanwhile CXMT — 7.67% of global DRAM in 2025, fourth-largest producer, and carrying no HBM project anywhere in its IPO prospectus — is worth $485B.

Nothing about HBM demand changed this week. What changed is the market's view of who collects the memory rent, and it changed on a retail-driven Star Market print rather than on a single unit of commodity DRAM competing with a single stack of HBM4. Commodity DRAM and HBM are not the same product, are not sold to the same buyer, and are not priced off the same cycle. This is a narrative repricing wearing a fundamental's clothes.
