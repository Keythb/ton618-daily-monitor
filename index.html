Equities · IPO · SPCX

The market priced SpaceX's $75B as a hyperscaler threat, not a chip windfall: silicon rallied, the cloud incumbents didn't.

SpaceX completed the largest IPO in history Friday — $75B raised at a ~$1.75T valuation, triple Saudi Aramco's 2019 record. The stock priced at $135, opened $150, tagged $176.52 intraday and settled near $166.84 (+23.6% incl. post-market) on 522M shares. Roughly 58% of revenue is Starlink; up to 30% of the float was steered to retail.

The derivative read mainstream coverage missed: the proceeds are earmarked for SpaceX's own data-center and compute buildout. The tape rewarded the merchant-silicon suppliers to that buildout — AMD +5.4%, Qualcomm +4.8% — while the cloud hyperscalers that would compete with an in-house SpaceX compute stack went nowhere or fell: MSFT +0.1%, ORCL −0.1%, AMZN −1.2%. That is the market pricing a new $75B capex entrant as additive to chips and subtractive to cloud rents.

Actionable: Treat SPCX day-1 levels ($150 open / $176.5 high) as the first support/resistance band; lock-up and S-1 use-of-proceeds detail are the next catalysts. The cleaner expression is the pair — long merchant silicon vs. the hyperscaler capex-moat trade — not chasing SPCX into a 23% pop.

Geopolitics · Energy · Defense

The peace dividend is being paid in three places at once: crude down, defense down, vol crushed.

President Trump signaled the Strait of Hormuz will "open to all" under a U.S.–Iran peace deal slated to be signed today — the formal close of the war that began with the Feb 28 strikes and cut Hormuz crossings ~70%. Markets repriced the war premium in unison Friday: USO −2.6%, the defense complex red across the board (ITA −1.0%, LMT −1.6%, NOC −0.6%, RTX −0.6%), and VIX collapsed from a 19.5 open to 17.7 as the S&P closed near a record 7,431.

The data the headline can't show: live OpenSky tracking over the Strait at mid-morning UTC showed just four aircraft — all civilian (Gulf Air, Qatari, Indian carriers), no military air activity — consistent with de-escalation rather than a fresh flashpoint. Credit confirms the all-clear: high-yield OAS sits at 2.78%, near cycle tights. FRED's WTI still reads $95 (data through Jun 8, pre-signing); the Friday tape is where the ~$95 war premium starts unwinding.

Actionable: A clean signing is a disinflationary tailwind into Wednesday — every dollar off crude eases the second-round CPI risk. Fade defense strength on confirmation; the asymmetric risk is a signing that slips, which re-bids oil and ITA in a single headline.
