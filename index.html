Macro · FX · Washington · DXJ EWJ

Washington co-signed a yen defense and paid for it in euros — the size is trivial, the signature is not

Japan's Ministry of Finance and the US Treasury jointly bought yen during New York hours on Friday 31 July, confirmed Monday morning in Tokyo by Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama. It is the first coordinated US–Japan yen operation since 1998. The yen had reached ¥163.99 on 23 Jul, its weakest since 1986; it trades near ¥156.40 after the confirmation, about 4.6% off the low. Estimated scale of the operation is ¥6–8.45tn.

The American contribution is small and the funding leg is the tell. A Reuters photograph of Treasury Secretary Bessent's notepad showed a $5–10bn yen purchase — at most 10–20% of the operation — and the New York Fed executed it by selling euros out of the Exchange Stabilization Fund through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. The US half creates no Treasury supply at all. Japan's half is the one that does: to fund a ¥11.73tn operation in May, Japan's Treasury holdings fell $66.7bn, mostly in short bills, out of $1.143tn held.

The whole event is priced in the hedge, not the equity. EWJ (unhedged) −0.06% pre-market against DXJ (yen-hedged) −2.33% — the same Japanese stocks, a 227bp wedge, and that wedge is the currency. The dollar investor who owned Japan unhedged lost nothing this morning; the one who paid to strip the yen out paid for the intervention. That is the cleanest available read on how much of the move the market thinks is real: 227bp in one session, with no equity de-rating attached.

Disclosure: our own FRED yen series is stale for this event. DEXJPUS last prints 163.71 on 24 Jul, a week before the operation, so every spot level above is wire reporting rather than an in-house series. The next FRED observation is the first independent confirmation we will have.

Actionable: EWJ outperforms DXJ over 5 sessions from the 3 Aug close tests whether the intervention holds. If that spread reverses, the market is calling ¥6–8.45tn a floor worth retesting rather than a line worth respecting.

That matters because it is the wrong signal into Friday. June payrolls were +57k against ~110k expected with May revised down to +129k and unemployment at 4.2%. Falling claims against a soft payroll trend is the exact combination that keeps a hike alive; a trigger telling us claims were rising would have argued for the other side of that book.

The generalisable lesson is about trigger construction, not about labor. A level set on a four-week average is a level that fires late by design, and the fund published it that way. Triggers on smoothed series need a companion condition on the unsmoothed one.
