Washington · Regulation · Stablecoins · GENIUS Act

While the crypto price capitulated, the crypto rulebook went live. The GENIUS Act's statutory deadline for issuer regulations — licensing, custody, capital, AML — falls July 18, and the Federal Register shows the agencies are actually shipping: six proposed rules from Treasury, the FDIC and the NCUA in the last 90 days. The stablecoin payment rails the market is ignoring are being built into federal law on a fixed clock.

The structural story runs opposite to the price. The GENIUS Act (enacted July 2025) directs regulators to finalize the operating rules for "permitted payment stablecoin issuers" — licensing, capital, custody, AML/CFT — by 18 July 2026. The Federal Register confirms the rulemaking is real and on schedule: in the last 90 days, Treasury proposed AML/CFT (24 Jun) and Customer Identification (22 Jun) rules, the FDIC proposed BSA/sanctions standards (5 Jun) and GENIUS issuer standards, and the NCUA proposed its implementing rule (18 May) — atop the SEC's January token-taxonomy interpretation clarifying digital commodities, stablecoins and digital securities.

The derivative read: this is regulated stablecoin infrastructure entering core finance on a deadline the price action is discounting to zero. The one piece still stuck is the broader market-structure bill — the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act passed the House 294–134 but sits in Senate Banking, snagged on the GENIUS provision letting intermediaries pay yield on stablecoins. Payment stablecoins are explicitly not securities under GENIUS, so the July 18 rules stand regardless. The beneficiary is the rails — exchanges, custodians, issuers — not the volatile spot price that dominates the tape.

Actionable: The 18 Jul rule drop is a dated, underpriced catalyst — own the infrastructure layer (COIN as the cleanest listed proxy), not the tokens. Watch for the final Treasury/FDIC issuer rules on or before 18 Jul; any slippage or a hawkish AML surprise is the risk. The Senate CLARITY Act markup is the bigger, later prize — unresolved into the fall.
