Washington · Regulation · Stablecoins

The stablecoin rulebook is being written into the Federal Register right now — a BSA/sanctions-screening regime for payment-stablecoin issuers that is a moat for compliant incumbents and a tax on new entrants.

U.S. stablecoin issuers face strict new KYC/AML obligations. The primary source is already public: a Proposed Rule, "Bank Secrecy Act and Sanctions Compliance Standards for FDIC-Supervised Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuers," was filed June 5 — the operational teeth on the GENIUS-Act framework, layering sanctions-screening and customer-identification duties onto regulated issuers. It lands alongside a market-structure turf war (CME suing the CFTC over Bitcoin perpetual-futures approval; Kalshi eyeing a $22B IPO).

The desk's read: this is structurally bullish for the rails, bearish for the long tail. Compliance cost is a moat — regulated issuers (Circle/USDC and bank-permitted entrants) gain share; unregulated and offshore supply gets squeezed, echoing exchanges signaling they will "raise the bar" on new listings. Near-term, USDT supply is already contracting (−$0.04B/30d) — a marginal drag on crypto inflows — but the through-line is the same one that makes regulated perps bullish: more institutional on-ramps for BTC, not fewer.

Actionable: Treat stablecoin formalization as a positive carry for the BTC institutional thesis, not a headwind — watch the comment-period close and any final rule for the compliance bar that decides which issuers survive. The CME/CFTC perps fight is the higher-beta catalyst: onshore Bitcoin perpetuals deepen U.S. derivatives liquidity regardless of who wins.
