This was not a hike pulled forward — the strip added one to every meeting through 2027

The obvious reading of Friday is that Warsh moved a hike into September. The curve says otherwise. September hike-25 went 29c → 54c (+25), but December went 36c → 47c (+11) rather than giving the ground up, and the far end moved with it: October-2027 7c → 20c (+13) and April-2027 8c → 18c (+10). A relocation empties the leg it comes from. Nothing was emptied. The market added policy tightening across eighteen months of meetings.

The exception is the one leg that should have moved most. October-2026 went 24c → 27c, up three cents — the smallest move on the near curve. The strip now prices a September hike, an October pause, and a second hike option in December: 54c, 27c, 47c. That is a coherent forecast only if the Fed hikes and then explicitly waits, which is not what a Chair who said recent prints "do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved" is describing.

The confirmations are missing where they should be loudest. Five-year-forward breakevens fell to 2.32 on 28 Aug from 2.35 — inflation compensation went down on a hawkish keynote, which is the market saying he will get it rather than that he needs to. 2s10s flattened 8bp to 0.39 from 0.47, the front end doing all the work. And VIX closed 14.43 on Friday and sits at 15.38 this morning. Gold took the hit instead: GLDGLD — SPDR Gold Shares. The largest physically backed gold ETF; a standard institutional gold proxy. −3.2% on Friday, its worst session of the month.

This desk called the keynote a non-event and was wrong by 20 cents. The correction is not that the Fed is more hawkish than we thought — it is that a Chair's first keynote carries information the eleventh does not, and the base rate for "no new information from a scheduled speech" does not apply to a debut.
