Following the US-Iran 60-day roadmap signed in Geneva (~June 19), CENTCOM reported 55 ships transiting Hormuz on June 21 even as Iran's military "declared" the strait closed — the tape sided with diplomacy. WTI has bled from $95 (June 8) to $84.65, and the defense complex sold off in sympathy: Lockheed −3.4% ($493.60), RTX −2.0% ($181.83). The same de-escalation is doing double duty — easing oil and relieving the inflation pressure on the Fed (see above).

The asymmetry: Persian-Gulf throughput is still down 95–99% versus February, and a 60-day MOU is a thin reed. A single tanker incident or a re-closure of Hormuz — which carries ~27% of seaborne crude — reprices oil +$10–15 in hours and snaps defense back. The market is paid to sell the war premium today but exposed to a violent re-pricing on any headline.

Actionable: The peace trade is consensus into the ~Aug 18 deal deadline. Cheap oil/defense convexity (or VIX) is the asymmetric hedge against a ceasefire breakdown — owning the tail is mispriced when everyone is short the premium.
