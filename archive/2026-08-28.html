Nvidia became OpenAI's landlord-of-last-resort and disclosed it in a filing nobody read

The house neocloud-credit trigger came due this morning asking one question — whether OpenAI reappears in NVIDIA's next 10-Q. It does, six times, and not as a customer. The 10-Q filed 26 August alongside the print discloses guarantees entered into in August 2026, capped at $105 billion, providing credit support on 20-year leases to an affiliate of OpenAI Group PBC covering roughly 4.25 gigawatts of IT load at SB Energy's PORTS campus in Pike County, Ohio, with an option NVIDIA holds at its sole discretion to backstop 3.8 further gigawatts. Total maximum gross guarantee exposure rises to $108.5 billion. The prior 10-Q, filed 20 May, does not contain the word OpenAI once, and uses the word guarantee five times against thirty-five today.

The derivative fact is the termination clause. The guarantees end, among other events, on “OpenAI achieving a satisfactory credit rating” — which is a disclosure that the counterparty does not have one now, and that NVIDIA's balance sheet is standing in for it. The risk-factor language is explicit that on an OpenAI insolvency NVIDIA “may assume the applicable lease” and carry 20-year obligations on a nine-building campus. Alongside it sit $99B of equity investments and $25B of equity investment commitments, and a separate arrangement under which NVIDIA agrees to purchase AI-cloud capacity that partners fail to sell to third parties. Vendor equity, vendor offtake and now vendor credit, in one filing.

The working-capital line moved with it. Receivables went $40.71B → $63.06B in one quarter, up 54.9% against revenue up 17.9%, taking days sales outstanding from 45.4 to 59.6. The honest counterweight is stated before the conclusion and it is substantial: top-customer concentration fell, one direct customer at 16% against two at 23% and 16% a year ago; finished goods declined to $6.86B from $8.77B while raw materials tripled to $11.34B, which is an input build for demand rather than unsold product; customer advances went $160M → $2.8B, so buyers are prepaying. None of that touches the guarantee, which is contingent, phases in from fiscal 2029, declines as OpenAI pays, and is indemnified in part. The point is not that the exposure is impaired. It is that a $105B contingent credit line to a private, unrated counterparty was disclosed on Wednesday and the complex spent Thursday trading the revenue guide — and then sold MRVL −5.7% on a double beat and a raised guide anyway.

The market cannot claim it repriced this. NVDA trades 228.96, +0.4%, 3.2% below its 236.54 52-week high, on the second session after a quarter that printed $96.2B revenue, +105.9% year on year at a 75.0% gross margin.

Actionable: The test is whether the 10-Q caps the post-print re-rate: NVDA does not close at or above its $236.54 52-week high on any session through 11 Sep 2026. A reclaim says the disclosure is priced as commercial exclusivity — the campus hosts NVIDIA silicon only — rather than as credit.
